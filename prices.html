Market prices

Price information is kept distinct from demand signals. Every price on this page shows the commodity, the unit, the market and the date, so a farmer can tell what the number actually means.

Latest prices

Prices come from the ShambaPulse database. Each record keeps its unit, market, date and source, so a number is never shown without its meaning.

Commodity Unit Market Date Price

Prices are shown with their unit, source and date in the full system. A price without a unit is not useful, so the unit is never left out.

Why the unit matters

A price for "maize" means nothing until you know whether it is per kilogram, per 90 kg bag, or per debe. ShambaPulse records the unit with every price, and duplicate feed imports are blocked by a stable key so the same price is not counted twice.

How to read a price

  • Commodity

    What is being sold, such as maize, beans or milk.

  • Unit

    How it is measured: a 90 kg bag, a litre, a debe.

  • Market

    Where the price was recorded. Prices differ from market to market.

  • Date

    When it was recorded. An old price may no longer hold.

Compare before you sell

  • By market

    Farmers compare prices across nearby markets before deciding where to sell.

  • By commodity

    Each commodity has its own unit, so prices are compared like with like.

  • By date

    The date beside every price shows how fresh the information is.

Tips before you sell

  1. Check the unit

    Make sure you are comparing the same bag, litre or debe.

  2. Check two markets

    A short trip can sometimes mean a better price, but count transport costs.

  3. Check the date

    Use the freshest price you can find before agreeing to sell.
